# Tracing a chain of title

The chain is built by joining instruments: the grantee of one is the grantor of the next.
Everything below is about that join and the ways it fails.

## 1. Walk backwards, from the most recent instrument

Start at the latest instrument conveying the parcel and take its grantor. Look that party up
as a grantee. The instrument you find is the previous link. Repeat.

Forwards is the wrong direction for an examination. It answers "what happened next", and the
question is "what does the present rest on".

## 2. When the join fails, say which kind of failure it is

Three things look identical at the point the walk stops, and they are not the same finding:

- The next instrument does not exist — the chain is genuinely at its root.
- It exists and was not found — a name variant, a wrong year, a search that ended early.
- It exists, was found, and cannot be read.

Only the third is established by evidence. The first is almost never established at all: see
`party/ada-renwick`, where the chain stops because the search stopped. Write down which one
you have.

## 3. An index entry is evidence that a document exists, and nothing else

The grantor–grantee index establishes that an instrument was recorded under a name in a year.
It does not say what the instrument conveyed, and a chain that treats an index entry as a
link has assumed the thing it was looking for. See `instrument/1961-indexed-conveyance`.

## 4. Do not let the instrument type do your reasoning

A quitclaim conveys whatever the grantor had, which may be nothing. A general warranty is a
covenant about the past, which is a promise and not a record. Neither denomination is
evidence about what the grantor actually held — see `instrument/1974-quitclaim-deed`.

## 5. Never write down an owner

Ownership is an entailment, evaluated at a moment, and it goes stale silently. See
`decisions/owner-is-not-a-class`. If somebody needs the answer, walk the chain and report
what the walk found, including where it stopped.
