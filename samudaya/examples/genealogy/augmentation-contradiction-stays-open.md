# A contradiction between records is a finding, not an error to fix

When two records attest incompatible facts, the corpus records both attestations and marks
the resulting claim `[open]`. It does not silently prefer the earlier record, the more
official one, or the one found first.

Published standards for genealogical proof ask for a reasonably exhaustive search and the
resolution of conflicting evidence — and resolution there means an argued account of why one
reading is better, written down. An unargued preference is not resolution; it is the conflict
disappearing. `[open]` is how the corpus holds a conflict that has not yet been argued, and a
node that carries one is doing its job rather than failing.
