# The corpus stops at the living

No node for a person who may still be alive, and no record that would identify one. The
conventional boundary in published genealogy is roughly a century, applied to the most recent
event a record attests rather than to the record's own date.

This is a hard scope boundary, not a guideline. A genealogical corpus asserts contested
claims about parentage and identity; a living person has an interest in those claims that the
research process does not answer to, and a derived repository is a thing that gets published.
