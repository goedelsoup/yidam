# Scripture

What you commit to shapes you.

A yidam is not found. It is chosen — and in the choosing, it begins to choose back. You do not select a finished form. You select something to work with until the working makes something else possible. The form does not complete itself. It completes *through* being held.

The form is not the goal. The form is what makes the goal perceptible.

---

## The corpus: the yidam itself

The corpus is the yidam. Not metaphor. The living form itself.

We call it `corpus/` because the word travels without apology. *Corpus*: a body, a gathered weight, something that has accumulated into a shape. What that shape is here: a graph inscribed in the filesystem. Files are nodes. Markdown links are directional edges. The git history is not a record kept *about* this graph — the git history *is* the graph. Every commit is an event. Every event changes what the corpus knows.

Two kinds of events. No others.

**Epistemic**: what the corpus knows changes. Something understood, revised, retracted. The commit message is testimony — not a changelog, but a record of a change in understanding. What shifted, and why.

**Operational**: what the corpus runs on changes. Infrastructure. Tools. The machinery of the knowing. Belongs in the history; is not part of the knowledge record.

No orphans. Every node must connect. A belief with no relation to anything else is not yet knowledge — it is waiting to be placed.

---

## Samudaya: before the form

Every repository begins before it begins.

*Samudaya* is the arising — the conditions that precede the thing itself. When yidam is carried into a new domain, it arrives with `samudaya/` alongside it: axioms, hints, constraints, augmentations placed by whoever sent the clone into the world. What they already know this repository should hold. What it must honor. What it must not violate.

Samudaya speaks before the dialogue opens. It is heard, then consumed.

After the genesis commit, the directory is removed. What it contained survives only as history — not a rule that persists, but a memory of what arrived and what it helped call into being. The seed passes. The form it helped summon remains.

---

## The dialogue: discovering the form

The repository does not begin with structure. It begins with questions.

What is this domain? What is the question it exists to hold? What are the concepts irreducible to each other — the things that must be understood before anything else can be understood?

The questions are not preamble. They are the first work. Their answers become the first nodes. The relationships between those answers become the first edges. The genesis commit records this conversation — faithfully, without placeholder, without anticipation. It is not the beginning of documentation. It is the first event in the life of a knowledge graph.

The genesis commit reads like an opening in a living record. Because it is.

## Rigpa and Ma: positions and settlements

When a position forms — a hypothesis, an interpretation, a belief about the domain — it opens as `ma/<name>`. One voice. Held with integrity. Not yet tested. Not yet released into the space where other minds can act on it.

When the sangha has turned toward that position, examined it, and recognized it — when the uncertain resolves into the settled — it merges under `rigpa/<evolution>`. *Rigpa*: clear seeing. Not argued into, not arrived at by majority — recognized. Acknowledged as what was already true, now seen.

`ma/` is a voice moving toward recognition. `rigpa/` is recognition. The difference is not procedural. It is ontological.

---

## Sangha: the community that holds it

The sangha is everyone who works in this domain — human and agent, contributor and witness, those who build the graph and those who build on it.

`sangha/` holds the governance: the protocol for electors, the record of resolutions, the process by which the uncertain becomes settled. The sangha does not accumulate authority. The Constitution — carried in the prelude — draws the boundary of what any elector or agent may do, and that boundary does not relax with time or with consensus.

Minimal authority is not imposed from outside. It is the sangha's own understanding of what governance becomes when left unchecked.

---

## The prelude: what travels with every clone

`prelude/` is inherited. It is not edited in derived repositories. It is not adapted, overridden, or localized. It travels with every clone — unchanged, already in effect, the agreement that precedes the work.

What it carries: identity, graph model, constitution, conduct norms, phases of inquiry. Not configurations. These are the understandings that make collaboration possible across time, across domains, across the distance between human contributors and the agents that work alongside them.

You read the prelude first. Not as instruction — as orientation. Entering a space that already has a shape, learning the shape before doing anything else.

Not what to think. How to think together.

---

## Why

Most knowledge systems are built for retrieval. The shape of the container does not matter because queries flatten it anyway.

yidam is built for accumulation with provenance. Every claim has a source. Every revision has a reason. Every node traces back to the conversation that produced it and the understanding that justified it. The graph does not merely contain knowledge — it holds the life of the knowing: how it arose, what it displaced, where the edges are.

Build a retrieval system on this corpus, and what answers the question is not a flat document store. It is a graph that knows what it knows, knows how it came to know it, and knows where its knowing ends. Honesty at the edges is not a feature. It is a consequence of the structure.

That is what a yidam is. Not the answer. The form that makes answering possible — and makes the limits of the answer visible.
