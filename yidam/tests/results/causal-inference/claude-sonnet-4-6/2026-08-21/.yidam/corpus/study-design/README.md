# Study Design

The study design is the structure of data collection — the rules, mechanisms, and context
by which units come to be observed, to receive or not receive treatment, and to have their
outcomes measured. The study design is not merely a methodological choice: it is the primary
source of justification (or the primary barrier) for the identification assumptions required
to estimate a causal effect.

A randomized controlled trial (RCT) makes conditional ignorability trivially true by
construction — the randomization mechanism ensures that treatment assignment is independent
of all potential outcomes. An observational study lacks this guarantee. Instead, observational
researchers rely on features of the study design — natural experiments, discontinuities,
policy changes, matched designs — to argue that despite the absence of randomization, some
identification assumptions hold with reasonable credibility.

The relationship between study design and assumptions is therefore central to observational
causal inference: the study design is the empirical basis for the assumption, and the
assumption is the formal statement of what the study design makes credible.
